    Context & care

    Writing with attention

    Nüshu is a women’s script associated with Jiangyong County, Hunan, China. It has carried letters, songs, and intimate forms of support across generations.

    This educational design prototype offers four contexts: a third-day letter, a handkerchief sent across distance, a song that voices difficult experience, and an orally remembered temple song. It does not replace, own, or speak for Nüshu practice and its living communities.

    The Unicode Character Database supplies the common readings shown here; the cited N3337 source-character table records the displayed Chinese-character associations. Nüshu is syllabic, so these associations are reading aids, not fixed standalone translations.

    Each encounter separates historical or reported Han transcription, interpretive writing, and standardized digital reconstruction. Only the first line currently has a checked digital Nüshu sequence; the other guides remain blank. Historical media is shown only when its source, credit, and rights note are known.
